Example Letter — After (Chapter 1–3 + 6 process, proof-bank facts only)

Fictional composite. Same writer (Sam R.), same skill set, drafted with the Chapter 3 prompt recipes against the Chapter 3 proof bank, employer names neutralized per Chapter 6.

Dear Ms. Okafor,

You're hiring a Customer Success Manager for nurse-scheduling software, and I've spent eight years being the user your product talks about — twelve-hour shifts, charge rotations, and every schedule mess a 32-bed unit can produce. That lived experience is what I'd bring to your customer conversations that no amount of corporate onboarding can teach.

The pattern in my background matches this job in miniature. When my unit adopted smart infusion pumps, I volunteered to pilot the rollout and trained twenty night-shift nurses on it — workflow, troubleshooting, and earning the room's trust one grumpy question at a time. I also built a shift-change handoff checklist that charge nurses kept using after I rotated off, so I know what it takes to make a process change stick with busy, skeptical people. And as a rotating charge nurse, I made the same daily coverage trade-offs your customers' charge nurses face every morning.

What I don't have is SaaS account-management experience, and I won't pretend otherwise. What I have instead is the product's actual end user's respect, plus eight years of showing up for twelve-hour shifts without a single call-off — which is the core of customer success whatever logo sits on the software.

I'd welcome the chance to talk about where scheduling pain really lives on the floor. Thank you for reading.

Sam R.
