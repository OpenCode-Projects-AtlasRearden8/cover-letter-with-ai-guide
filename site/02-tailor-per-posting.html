The cheap tailoring trap

Two bad modes exist: zero tailoring (one generic letter blasted everywhere) and cover-letter theater (so much flattery per posting that it takes 45 minutes each and you burn out after three). This chapter gives you the middle path: a 10–15 minute per-posting process.

Step 1 — Extract the posting's own language (3 min)

Paste the job posting into your notes and pull out, by hand (do not delegate your reading to AI yet):

  • The title they actually used (not yours — theirs)
  • The three things repeated most in responsibilities and qualifications — repetition is the posting telling you what it really wants
  • Any tool, system, or certification named
  • The stack or setting: hospital, clinic, remote, SaaS, payer, vendor

Step 2 — Map your evidence to their repeats (3 min)

For each repeated want, write one bullet from your own history that answers it — a duty, a number, a story. If you cannot fill even one of the three, pause: this posting may genuinely be a stretch, and that is useful to know before you spend the hour.

Do this by hand. Physical act, real recall. This is also your honesty check: anything you cannot recall an example for, you cannot claim.

Step 3 — Now bring AI in (5 min)

Only now do you use AI, and only with the sanitized, structured material: your bullets, the posting's repeated wants, the required keywords. The prompt recipe is in Chapter 3, but the shape is:

"Here are three things this job asks for, and three true bullets from my background. Draft a 200-word letter whose one argument is [pick from Ch. 1]. Neutralize any employer names as 'a 400-bed community hospital' or 'a mid-size software team.' Ask me three questions if any bullet is unclear before drafting."

That last line matters. Asking the AI to question you first produces a far better draft — it forces the specifics out of you instead of letting the AI invent filler.

Step 4 — The exaggeration guard (2 min)

Read the draft with one question per sentence: "Is this verbatim true of me?"

Common drift to fix:

AI draft saysFix to
"led a team of 12" (you were charge nurse for shifts)"served as charge nurse, coordinating flow for a 12-bed unit"
"expert in data analytics" (you made shift reports in Excel)"comfortable with Excel reporting and unit-level metrics"
"passionate about healthcare technology" (you used an EHR daily)"six years of daily EHR use, with an eye for what slows clinicians down"

Downgrade verbs (led → coordinated, expert → experienced), add the qualifier that makes it true, or cut it. A slightly weaker true sentence beats a stronger inflated one — because interviews probe the strong ones.

Step 5 — Keyword pass (1 min)

Check the posting's exact phrases appear naturally where true: "patient experience," "cross-functional," "chart review," whatever they use. ATS systems and skimming humans both look for echoed language. Echo it exactly; do not creatively paraphrase keywords you actually have.

Step 6 — Voice pass (1 min)

Read aloud. Fix any sentence you would never say. Swap "leverage" for "use." Cut "Furthermore." Ship it.

What tailoring is not

  • It is not claiming the posting's requirements you lack.
  • It is not adopting a new personality each time.
  • It is not rewriting from scratch every time — your proof bank (Chapter 3) is reusable; only the argument and echo change.
